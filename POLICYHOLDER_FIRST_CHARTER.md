## Preamble

This Charter establishes the governance principles for a **policyholder-first reciprocal insurance exchange** serving coastal families. It is designed to prevent the structural extraction that characterizes the current insurance market and to return value to the families who bear the actual risk.

A reciprocal insurer is owned by its policyholders (called **Subscribers**). Surplus belongs to Subscribers, not shareholders. This Charter enforces that principle with specific, measurable rules — not aspirational language.

This Charter is also a **curriculum document**. Every provision is designed to be teachable to a 13-year-old. If a provision cannot be explained simply, it is written wrong.

---

## Article I — Core Principles

### 1.1 Non-Extraction Mandate
The Exchange exists to pay claims efficiently and return surplus to Subscribers. It does not exist to accumulate capital for executives, agents, or outside investors.

### 1.2 Transparency as Default
All financials, compensation figures, combined ratios, loss ratios, and investment returns shall be published in real-time on a public dashboard. Opacity is a form of extraction.

### 1.3 Policyholder Sovereignty
Subscribers retain ultimate authority over major decisions. Management proposes; Subscribers dispose.

### 1.4 Antifragility Mandate
The Exchange shall be structured to grow stronger from adverse events — not collapse under them. Claims surges are design inputs, not disasters.

### 1.5 Skin in the Game
Every executive and board member shall hold a personal policy with the Exchange for the duration of their service.

## Article II — Surplus Distribution Formula

### 2.1 Mandatory Surplus Return
After maintaining the **Minimum Solvency Reserve** (see 2.2), **80% of annual surplus** shall be distributed to Subscribers within 90 days of fiscal year close.

Distribution formula:  
`Subscriber Share = (Subscriber Premiums Paid / Total Premiums Collected) × Distributable Surplus`

### 2.2 Minimum Solvency Reserve
The Exchange shall maintain a reserve equal to **150% of the prior year's largest single catastrophe loss**. Reserves above this threshold are distributable surplus.

### 2.3 No Retained Surplus Accumulation
The Exchange shall not retain surplus beyond the Minimum Solvency Reserve for more than **3 consecutive years** without a Subscriber vote approving a specific capital deployment plan.

### 2.4 Claims-First Priority
In any year where the combined ratio exceeds 100%, the surplus distribution is deferred — but credited to Subscriber accounts as **deferred surplus certificates** redeemable in future profitable years.

## Article III — Executive & Agent Compensation Caps

### 3.1 Executive Compensation Cap
Total compensation (salary + bonus + equity + benefits) for any executive shall not exceed **8× the median annual premium paid by Subscribers** in the prior year.

*Example: If median annual premium = $4,000, maximum executive comp = $32,000. This forces executives to keep premiums low to protect their own compensation.*

### 3.2 Agent Commission Cap
Agent commissions shall not exceed **8% of first-year premium** and **3% of renewal premium**. No contingent commissions, volume bonuses, or override arrangements that create incentives to oversell or churn policies.

### 3.3 No Placement Fee Extraction
The Exchange shall not pay placement fees, fronting fees, or management fees to affiliated entities that have not been approved by a majority Subscriber vote.

### 3.4 Compensation Transparency
All compensation above $50,000/year shall be published on the Transparency Dashboard within 30 days of payment.

## Article IV — Subscriber Rights

### 4.1 Veto Rights
Subscribers holding **15% or more of total annual premiums** may call a special vote on any management decision. A simple majority vote of participating Subscribers is binding.

### 4.2 Claim Appeal Rights
Every denied claim shall include:
- The specific policy provision cited
- A plain-language explanation (8th-grade reading level)
- An independent appraisal option at the Exchange's expense if the claim exceeds $5,000

### 4.3 Policy Language Rights
All policy documents shall be written at an **8th-grade reading level** or lower. Exclusions shall be listed on Page 1, not buried in endorsements.

### 4.4 Data Rights
Subscribers own their own claims history and risk data. The Exchange shall provide a machine-readable export within 48 hours of any request at no charge.

### 4.5 Right to Inspect
Any Subscriber may request a full financial audit of the Exchange's accounts. The Exchange shall fund one independent audit per year, available publicly.

## Article V — Governance Structure

### 5.1 Attorney-in-Fact (AIF)
The Exchange shall be managed by an Attorney-in-Fact (AIF) appointed by and accountable to the Subscribers. The AIF may be removed by a 60% Subscriber vote at any time.

### 5.2 Advisory Board
A seven-member Advisory Board shall include:
- 3 Subscriber representatives (elected annually)
- 2 independent risk/actuarial experts
- 1 community resilience representative (rotating, nominated by Subscriber groups)
- 1 legal/compliance representative

### 5.3 Board Compensation
Advisory Board members shall receive compensation not to exceed **2× the median annual Subscriber premium** per year. No board member may receive contingent compensation tied to Exchange profitability.

### 5.4 Conflict of Interest
Any AIF, board member, or executive with a financial interest in a vendor, reinsurer, or contractor doing business with the Exchange must disclose it publicly and recuse from related votes.

## Article VII — Anti-Extraction Mechanisms

### 7.1 Related-Party Transaction Ban
The Exchange shall not do business with any entity in which an executive, board member, or their immediate family holds more than 5% equity — without a Subscriber vote.

### 7.2 Reinsurance Transparency
All reinsurance treaties shall be filed publicly within 30 days of execution. Ceding commissions and profit-sharing arrangements shall be disclosed.

### 7.3 Investment Constraint
Exchange investments shall be limited to:
- U.S. Treasury securities
- Investment-grade municipal bonds
- FDIC-insured deposits
- Bitcoin (up to 5% of reserves, for inflation protection)

No derivatives, hedge funds, private equity, or leveraged instruments.

### 7.4 No Offshore Reinsurance Arbitrage
The Exchange shall not use offshore captive reinsurers or special purpose vehicles to extract premium from Subscribers while appearing to retain risk locally.

## Appendix A — Key Definitions

**Subscriber:** A policyholder who has signed the Subscriber Agreement, entitling them to voting rights and surplus distributions.

**Surplus:** Premium collected minus claims paid minus operating expenses minus reinsurance costs minus the Minimum Solvency Reserve.

**Combined Ratio:** (Claims Incurred + Operating Expenses) / Premium Earned. Below 100% = profitable; above 100% = unprofitable.

**Float:** Premium held by the insurer between collection and claim payment. Investable; a key source of insurance company profit.

**Extraction:** Any mechanism that transfers value from Subscribers to non-Subscribers (executives, agents, investors) beyond their legitimate compensation.

**Antifragile:** A system that gains strength from stress, volatility, or adverse events rather than merely surviving them.

## Appendix C — Law & Economics: Where Truths Become Lies

This Charter exists because legal and economic systems have been systematically captured by interests that profit from policyholder confusion. The following principles, drawn from the AUBIEETERNAL Law & Economics curriculum, inform every provision of this Charter.

### C.1 The Noble Lie Problem
Many insurance industry practices are justified by comforting myths: "we deny claims to protect all policyholders," "complex language is legally necessary," "regulators protect consumers." Each of these contains partial truth and deliberate distortion. This Charter rejects noble lies and requires plain-English transparency.

### C.2 Regulatory Capture Prevention
George Stigler's theory of regulatory capture predicts that regulated industries eventually control their regulators. The insurance industry in Florida and other coastal states is a textbook example: industry lobbyists helped draft the anti-concurrent causation clauses, sublimits, and appraisal barriers now embedded in state law.

**This Charter's response:** Subscriber veto rights (Article IV.1), mandatory public compensation disclosure (Article VI), and a ban on related-party transactions (Article VII.1) are direct countermeasures against regulatory capture at the company level.

### C.3 Narrative Economics Awareness
Robert Shiller demonstrated that economic narratives spread virally and cause real harm. The "insurance protects you" narrative allows extractive products to persist. This Charter fights narrative with transparency: real-time public dashboards (Article VI) make the actual combined ratio visible to every Subscriber every day.

### C.4 The Law as a Tool — Used Both Ways
Legal mechanisms can extract or protect. The same arbitration clause that traps a policyholder can protect a cooperative from frivolous litigation. This Charter uses legal architecture defensively:
- Subscriber veto rights as a legal shield against management overreach
- Plain-English requirements as a legal obligation on the Exchange
- Independent audit rights as a legal guarantee of transparency

### C.5 Bitcoin as Design Model
Bitcoin's proof-of-work consensus mechanism is the most successful capture-resistant economic system ever built. The cost of attack exceeds the benefit. This Charter applies the same principle: making extraction more expensive than honest operation through compensation caps, surplus formulas, and real-time public accountability.

## Steelman This Charter

**Strongest argument FOR:**  
Structural rules (compensation caps, surplus formulas, veto rights) are the only reliable way to prevent extraction in insurance. Aspirational mission statements don't survive the first profitable year. Hard numbers do.

**Strongest argument AGAINST:**  
Rigid compensation caps may prevent the Exchange from hiring actuarial talent needed to price risk accurately — leading to underpricing, insolvency, and harming the very Subscribers it was designed to protect.

**Resolution:**  
The compensation formula is pegged to median Subscriber premium — if talent costs rise, premiums can be adjusted with full transparency. The Subscriber veto right prevents runaway premium increases while maintaining the cap's integrity.
